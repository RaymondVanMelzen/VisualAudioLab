# Visual Audio Lab: Product Vision

## Vision

Make the expressive power of audio programming approachable through a visual, hands-on environment - one that helps people create, explore, and understand sound without requiring them to give up control of their tools or their work.

## Mission

Visual Audio Lab gives musicians, sound designers, returning programmers, and DSP students a visual way to build and learn audio systems. It draws inspiration from the immediacy of classic programming environments, the clarity of visual dataflow, and the flexibility of modular audio systems.

The product is designed for people to use on their own computers, on Windows and macOS, with an offline-first experience. It is open source, with no subscription, cloud dependency, or AI dependency.

## Goals

- **Make visual patching the center of the experience.** Help users express audio ideas by connecting visible components and following how sound moves through a patch.
- **Lower the barrier to audio programming.** Make experimentation possible for people who may not be professional programmers, while retaining depth for people who want to explore DSP.
- **Support making and learning.** Serve creative work and technical exploration without treating either as secondary.
- **Respect user independence.** Keep the core experience usable without a network connection, subscription, cloud service, or AI service.
- **Support the named desktop platforms.** Build for Windows and macOS as first-class destinations.
- **Keep the project open.** Develop Visual Audio Lab as open-source software, consistent with the goal of user independence and transparency.

## Target users

Visual Audio Lab is for people interested in making or understanding audio through visual programming:

- **Former programmers** who want to return to building things in a familiar, exploratory way, without needing to start with a conventional code-first workflow.
- **Hobby musicians** who want to experiment with sound and audio systems as part of their creative practice.
- **Sound designers** who want a visual environment for exploring and shaping audio ideas.
- **DSP students** who want to investigate signal-processing concepts by working with visible audio structures.

These are overlapping audiences, not separate product tiers. The experience should invite beginners to start experimenting while remaining worthwhile for users who bring programming, musical, or DSP knowledge.

## Product positioning

Visual Audio Lab is a cross-platform desktop environment for visual audio programming. It combines the approachable, direct feel associated with classic programming tools such as Turbo Pascal and Visual Basic with the visual-flow clarity of LabVIEW and Pure Data, and the creative flexibility of modular audio systems.

Its defining interaction is visual patching: users work with connected audio structures rather than relying solely on text-based programming. The product is intended for creative exploration and learning, with an offline-first, open-source foundation. It is not positioned around a subscription, cloud service, or AI dependency.

## Key differentiators

- **Visual-first audio programming:** Audio systems are represented as patches users can inspect and work with visually.
- **Familiar, approachable inspiration:** The product draws on classic programming environments and visual dataflow tools to make technical ideas feel hands-on.
- **Creative and educational appeal:** The same visual approach is relevant to sound making, sound design, and learning DSP.
- **Desktop and offline-first:** The product is intended for Windows and macOS and is designed not to depend on cloud access for its core experience.
- **Open and independent:** Open-source distribution, no subscription, and no AI dependency are foundational product principles rather than optional add-ons.

## Success criteria

Visual Audio Lab is succeeding when:

- People in each of the named audiences can recognize a meaningful way to use it for their creative or learning goals.
- Users can understand a patch by looking at its visual structure and can express audio ideas through visual patching.
- New or returning users feel able to begin experimenting, while experienced users find enough conceptual depth to explore audio programming and DSP.
- The application remains useful as a desktop tool without requiring an internet connection, a subscription, a cloud service, or an AI service.
- Windows and macOS users can approach the product as supported audiences, and the project remains available as open-source software.

These criteria describe product outcomes; they do not prescribe numeric targets or a specific implementation roadmap.
